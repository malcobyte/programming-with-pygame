# Week 13 Rubric: Design & Planning

## Scoring categories

### 1. Design Document Completeness & Clarity

- **Emerging** — `DESIGN.md` missing sections, or so vague the game
  can't be visualized from reading it ("a fun adventure game").
- **Developing** — All sections present but generic or contradictory
  (e.g. the pitch and the core mechanics table describe different
  games).
- **Proficient** — Every section is specific and consistent; someone
  who has never heard the idea could describe the game back after
  reading it.
- **Advanced** — Same, plus the Core Mechanics table is genuinely
  minimal — nothing in it could be cut without breaking the game.

### 2. Scope Realism

- **Emerging** — Core Mechanics list is large (6+ substantial systems)
  with no acknowledgment that this exceeds two 3-hour sprints.
- **Developing** — Some scoping attempted, but the "cut it in half"
  question (from the worked example) wasn't seriously engaged with —
  answer is missing or superficial.
- **Proficient** — Core Mechanics list is realistically buildable in
  two sprints; Stretch Goals are genuinely optional, not disguised
  requirements.
- **Advanced** — Explicitly names things cut *entirely* from a bigger
  original idea (not just moved to stretch goals), showing real
  scoping judgment, not just optimism.

### 3. Mapping to Course Skills

- **Emerging** — Skill column is blank, or names skills inconsistent
  with what the mechanic actually requires.
- **Developing** — Most rows correctly mapped, but at least one
  mechanic depends on something not actually covered in Weeks 1–12
  without a plan for how to learn it.
- **Proficient** — Every Core Mechanic correctly maps to a specific
  skill from Weeks 1–12.
- **Advanced** — Mechanic list is thoughtfully assembled *from* known
  skills (built from what's possible) rather than skills being
  retrofitted onto an idea that was designed without constraints.

### 4. Gray-box Prototype

- **Emerging** — Prototype missing or doesn't run.
- **Developing** — Prototype runs but doesn't actually represent the
  design doc's core loop (e.g. just the reference skeleton, unedited).
- **Proficient** — Prototype is rough but demonstrably tests the
  actual core loop from `DESIGN.md`.
- **Advanced** — Prototype revealed something (fun, not fun, harder
  than expected) that visibly influenced a change to `DESIGN.md`
  before moving on.

## Signs of struggle

| Observed behavior | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Pitch requires "and" to describe | The idea is still two ideas fused together — a sign the scoping conversation needs another round before filling out the rest of the template. |
| Core Mechanics table has 8+ rows | Classic overscoping — very common on a first big personal project. Don't let this slide into Sprint 1; it will not fit in two sessions. |
| Every mechanic maps to "Week 8 — classes" | May indicate the student is reaching for the most recently-learned tool for everything rather than the right tool — worth asking "could this be a plain list/dict instead?" |
| Prototype is literally the unedited starter file | The gray-boxing step was skipped rather than attempted — this is a checkpoint gate, not optional; don't let Sprint 1 start without it. |
| Prototype reveals the idea isn't fun, but `DESIGN.md` wasn't updated | The whole point of prototyping *before* committing was missed — walk through why the prototype exists and revisit the design doc together. |

## Suggested next step by score pattern

- **Weak on Category 1-2 (doc/scope):** Don't let the student start
  Sprint 1 with an unrealistic plan — this is the highest-leverage
  moment in the whole capstone to intervene. Revisit the worked
  example's "cut it in half" question together, applied to their own
  idea specifically.
- **Strong on 1-2, weak on Category 3 (skill mapping):** The plan is
  reasonable but may be harder to execute than it looks. Walk through
  each row and ask "show me where you did this in an earlier week."
- **Weak on Category 4 (prototype):** This step exists to catch bad
  ideas cheaply — insist on it even if the design doc looks solid on
  paper. Some things only reveal themselves once played.
- **Advanced across the board:** Ready for Sprint 1. Make sure the
  Two-Sprint Plan section genuinely reflects "riskiest things first" —
  ask which row they're least sure will work, and confirm that's
  what's planned for early in Week 14.
