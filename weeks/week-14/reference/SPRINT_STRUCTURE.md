# Sprint 1: How to Structure This Session

This week is different from every week so far — there's no new pygame
concept and no co-built example. You already know everything you need
to build your Minimum Viable Version; this session is about applying
it independently.

## Suggested time breakdown (for a ~3 hour session)

**First 15 minutes — reconnect.**
Re-read `project/DESIGN.md`. Re-run your Week 13 gray-box prototype as
a reminder of where you left off. Don't start typing new code yet.

**Build in order of *risk*, not order of *fun*.**
Look at your Core Mechanics table. Build the row you're *least*
confident will work first — not the row you're most excited about.
If a mechanic turns out to be harder than expected, you want to find
that out at hour 1, not hour 3.

TALK: why is this backwards from how most people want to work? What
would go wrong if you built all the fun/easy parts first and saved the
uncertain part for last?

**Midpoint check-in.**
Around the halfway mark, stop and compare what's built against
`DESIGN.md`. Are you on track for everything in Sprint 1's plan? If
not, that's fine — this is exactly what the Sprint Log is for.

**Last 15 minutes — log, don't just stop.**
Before you finish, fill in Sprint 1's part of `DESIGN.md`'s Sprint Log
section: what actually got built, what's still open, and anything
your plan got wrong. Future-you (in Week 15) will thank present-you.

## When you get stuck

This is normal and expected — it's not a sign you're doing something
wrong. See this week's debugging resources, and try, in order:

1. Read the actual error message, slowly, out loud.
2. Add a `print()` statement near where you think the problem is, and
   check whether the values match what you expected.
3. Explain what the code *should* be doing, out loud, to your
   instructor (or a rubber duck, or anyone) — often the bug surfaces
   before they say anything back.
4. If you're still stuck after a genuine attempt, that's the right
   moment to ask for help — not a failure.
