# Week 16 Rubric: Presentation, Playtest, Reflection

## Scoring categories

### 1. Playtest Execution

- **Emerging** — No playtest run, or the student explained/helped
  throughout instead of observing.
- **Developing** — Playtest run, but notes are vague impressions
  ("they seemed to like it") rather than specific observed moments.
- **Proficient** — Playtest run with minimal interference; notes
  captured specific, concrete observations during play, not
  reconstructed afterward.
- **Advanced** — Notes show real attentiveness — specific moments of
  hesitation, delight, or confusion tied to specific parts of the
  game.

### 2. Responsiveness to Feedback

- **Emerging** — No change made after the playtest.
- **Developing** — A change was made, but it isn't clearly connected
  to anything observed or said during the playtest.
- **Proficient** — At least one change is directly and traceably
  connected to a specific playtest observation or answer.
- **Advanced** — The change meaningfully improves the game, and the
  student can explain why it addresses what was learned, not just
  what changed.

### 3. Reflection Quality

- **Emerging** — Reflection missing, or answers are generic
  ("it was fun," "I learned a lot") with no specifics.
- **Developing** — Some prompts answered specifically, others vague.
- **Proficient** — All four prompts answered with specific references
  to real moments, weeks, or bugs from the course.
- **Advanced** — Reflection shows genuine, thoughtful self-assessment
  — including honest acknowledgment of struggle, not just successes.

### 4. Presentation / Communication

- **Emerging** — Can't explain how their own game works, or the demo
  doesn't run.
- **Developing** — Can demo the game but struggles to explain specific
  code decisions or connect them to `DESIGN.md`.
- **Proficient** — Confidently demos the game and explains the
  reasoning behind key decisions, referencing the design doc and
  sprint log.
- **Advanced** — Can also speak to what changed from the original plan
  and why — the full arc from Week 13's pitch to the finished game.

## Signs of struggle

| Observed behavior | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Playtest notes are just "they liked it" | Specific behavioral observation wasn't practiced — redo a short playtest together, this time narrating out loud what you're both noticing in the moment. |
| No change made after the playtest | Either the feedback didn't surface anything actionable (worth double-checking the questions asked), or the student didn't know how to translate an observation into a code change — help bridge that gap explicitly with one example. |
| Reflection is short or generic | Same pattern as Week 8's checkpoint reflection — if it's still thin here, have a spoken conversation about the prompts first, then have them write from that conversation. |
| Can't explain their own code during the demo | A sign parts of the project were built by pattern-matching rather than understanding — not a failure, but worth noting for what to reinforce if this student continues past this course. |
| Playtest never happened | This is the one checkpoint that can't be faked or skipped without noticing — if time ran out, prioritize scheduling it before calling the course complete. |

## Suggested next step by score pattern

- **Weak on Category 1-2 (playtest/response):** These two skills — get
  real feedback, then actually act on it — are arguably the most
  transferable thing in this entire course, useful for anything the
  student builds after this. Worth insisting on redoing properly
  rather than checking the box.
- **Weak on Category 3 (reflection):** Sit down together and talk
  through the four prompts as a conversation before asking for
  written answers — some students reflect better out loud first.
- **Weak on Category 4 (presentation):** Practice the demo once,
  informally, before the "real" presentation — nerves and unfamiliarity
  with narrating your own work are common and improve with one
  rehearsal.
- **Strong across the board:** The course is complete. Use the "what
  do you want to learn next" reflection prompt as the actual starting
  point for whatever comes after this — that answer is real signal
  about where genuine interest is.
